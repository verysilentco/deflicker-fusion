# FlickerScope & Flicker Fusion

A physically grounded flicker **meter** and a flicker **remover** for high-speed (slow-motion) video
shot under mains-powered lighting. Working draft — results tables are regenerated by `bench/run.py`.

## 1. Problem

Slow-motion capture (120–960 fps) samples the light output of mains-powered lamps faster than the
lamps are steady. LED drivers, fluorescent ballasts and even filaments modulate at twice the mains
frequency (100/120 Hz) and its harmonics, so frames alternate between bright and dark. The artefact is

- **multiplicative** (it scales scene radiance), in **linear light**;
- **periodic but aliased**: 100 Hz at 240 fps is 5/12 cycle/frame, and its harmonics fold back onto
  every multiple of 1/12 — the visible "period-6" pulse in a 240-fps clip of 50 Hz lighting is the
  aliased *second* harmonic, while the fundamental is nearly invisible in a frame-wide average;
- **regional**: each light circuit has its own footprint, depth and phase; objects lit by other
  sources (a person, a chain) do not share it;
- **row-shifted** under a rolling shutter;
- **deep**: we measured 87 % IEEE-1789 Percent Flicker in a shooting-range clip — the darkest frames
  receive ~14× less light than the brightest, so brightening them amplifies noise ~14×;
- **mixed with real events**: a muzzle flash is a transient *additive* light, not flicker.

Generic ("blind") deflickering — global gain matching (ffmpeg `deflicker`), temporal filtering,
or neural-atlas methods (Lei et al., CVPR 2023) — models none of this.

## 2. FlickerScope: measuring flicker

Model, per pixel along motion trajectories, in linear light:

    Y(x, t) = S(x, t) · G(x, t)

with `S` the steady scene (changing only slowly or by motion) and `G` the illumination flicker gain.

1. **Linearise** (inverse sRGB) and compute luminance; mark clipped pixels.
2. **Optical flow** (RAFT) on *illumination-normalised* frames (each frame divided by a heavily blurred
   copy of its luminance), so flicker does not bias motion estimates.
3. **Motion-compensated fundamental detection**: spectra of the frame-to-frame change of log luminance
   *along optical flow*, averaged over 12×12 tiles and whitened per tile, with the difference operator's
   gain `2·sin(πf)` divided out. Moving content is followed by the flow and cancels; illumination
   change does not. (Spectra of raw tile brightness locked onto camera motion: after a 25-frame
   detrend, motion energy near a 25-frame period passes untouched — a 1.2 %-flicker clip read 35 %.)
   A clip counts as periodic above a strength of 25 (genuine flicker scored ≥ 160 in validation). The
   true fundamental is recovered even when it is weak in frame-wide averages.
4. **Aliased harmonic set**: harmonics h·f0 are folded into [0, ½] cycle/frame and de-duplicated.
   The fit window is ±K frames with K = 1.5 × the longest component period (4 ≤ K ≤ 20); a component
   whose period exceeds the window is dropped (indistinguishable from the window's linear trend).
   Harmonics are then admitted in order only while the window's design matrix stays well conditioned
   (condition number ≤ 20): near Nyquist the aliases bunch up (f0 = 0.495 puts the 3rd, 5th and 7th
   harmonics at 0.485, 0.475, 0.465) and a 41-frame window cannot separate them (condition 1000+).
   Genuine lamp harmonic sets score 1–2.5.
   **Support check**: if most per-pixel fits have fewer aligned samples than coefficients (flow cannot
   track rain, spray or confetti), the fit interpolates the samples exactly — zero residual, no noise
   floor to debias against, coefficients of ±50 in log units — so the clip falls back to the frame-level
   (aperiodic) model, which needs only the local trend. A total harmonic amplitude cap of e^±4 per
   pixel is a backstop (genuine fits peak at 3.2).
5. **Per-pixel, per-frame robust fit** of `log Y` over flow-aligned neighbours (±K frames, K ≈ 1.5 ×
   the longest component period): local linear trend + Fourier series at the aliased components.
   Normal equations are pooled over a small spatial window; IRLS starts with Huber weights and finishes
   with Tukey's biweight, so gross outliers (a flash) get zero weight. Harmonic amplitudes are
   **debiased against the residual noise floor**, so a clean video measures ≈ 0 %.
6. **Aperiodic flicker**: the *spatially smooth* part of the residual (illumination is smooth across
   the image, sensor noise is not), soft-thresholded against its noise level. Never extrapolated into
   pixels clipped in that frame.
7. **Events**: aperiodic deviations far outside a pixel's usual fluctuation. Anything more than +28 %
   beyond the flicker model is always an event; smaller deviations are events unless they recur as
   many separate episodes (model misfit). Event regions grow into adjacent deviating or clipped pixels.
   Events are excluded from the gain.
8. **Frame-level events**: when more than a quarter of a frame is an event (a muzzle flash lights
   the whole scene, faintly far from the source), the entire frame is an event — its gain is the
   periodic lamp model only. Per-pixel thresholds would split the flash's smooth falloff into patches.
9. **Starved frames**: where a frame's fit has too few valid aligned samples, its harmonic
   coefficients are blended toward neighbouring frames' (weighted by their support). Lamp flicker is
   stable, so this is safe; well-supported frames keep their own fit, so moving edges don't smear.
10. **Chronically clipped pixels**: a pixel that saturates in every bright phase never shows the fit
    how bright those phases are, so its gain is underestimated (in Fusion this made such pixels blink
    white once per light cycle). Where a pixel is clipped in a large share of a *local* time window
    (local, because the camera may move), its gain is filled from its unclipped surroundings — the same
    lamp lights both and the gain field is smooth across a surface.
11. **Arithmetic-mean normalisation**: a log-domain Fourier fit normalises `G` to its *geometric* mean.
    Perceived light is the *arithmetic* mean, which for deep flicker is far higher (at 87 % modulation,
    ~38 % higher). Each pixel's gain is renormalised so its arithmetic mean over a full light cycle is 1
    (cycle-average of `exp(waveform)` from that frame's coefficients, smoothed over time). Without this,
    the deeper a region flickers the darker its corrected level — an uneven tonal shift that measured
    7.3 → 1.85 (×10⁻³ linear) against the original's cycle-averaged appearance once fixed.
12. **Scores** of the gain `G`, per pixel: IEEE 1789 **Percent Flicker** `(max−min)/(max+min)` (2nd/98th
   percentiles over time) and **Flicker Index**. Video score = visibility-weighted mean (near-black and
   clipped pixels down-weighted); plus **coverage** (area above 3 %), **peak** (95th percentile),
   frequency (cycles/frame and playback Hz), and the list of event frames.

Outputs: headline Flicker %, per-pixel maps (strength, phase), a spatio-temporal cube table, the gain
field `G(x, t)` and the event mask — which drive the remover.

## 3. Flicker Fusion: removing flicker

1. **Gain at full resolution**: edge-aware (guided) upsampling of `G`, guided by a first-pass
   deflickered luminance.
2. **Fusion**: for each frame, every neighbour within ±R frames is warped onto it (occlusion-checked
   flow) and divided by its own gain — each is an estimate of the steady scene at that frame. They are
   merged with weights combining:
   - **signal-to-noise** — a neighbour captured under bright light carries far less amplified noise
     than a trough frame brightened 5–14×, so dark frames are rebuilt mostly from bright ones
     (this *reduces* grain instead of amplifying it);
   - **clipping** — a sample saturated in its own frame is rejected; a clipped pixel of the current
     frame is replaced only if the clipping is explained by flicker (the neighbour, re-lit to this
     frame's light level, would clip too), which recovers lamp highlights blown in bright phases;
   - **two-band robustness** — tolerant on brightness (gain estimates are least certain where the
     correction gap is largest), strict on structure (a structural mismatch means misalignment);
   - **events** — never borrowed across, never imported from a neighbour.
3. **Additive light at events**: a flash adds light (`I = S·G + E`); dividing by `G` would dim it
   with the lamps. At event pixels only the lamps' share is removed: `I − S_steady·(G − 1)`.
4. **Full-resolution gain refinement** (pass 2): per pixel, the periodic part of
   `log Y − log S₁ − log G_coarse` over aligned neighbours (constant term absorbs errors in the pass-1
   estimate `S₁`; unreliable samples imputed with a first periodic fit's prediction — judging outliers
   against the median instead would reject exactly the samples that carry a large correction; Wiener
   shrinkage; 3×3 guided smoothing) resolves thin or sharply bounded objects that flicker differently
   (a fixture rim, a silhouette edge). Fusion is then repeated with the refined gain.
5. **Frame-level events, phase-matched**: flash frames are rebuilt last, from their calm neighbours'
   finished outputs (flow-aligned) as the steady scene. When the flicker is exactly periodic (every
   aliased component a multiple of 1/P — 100 Hz at 240 fps gives P = 12), the lamp light during the
   event is read from frame t−P, which saw the same lamp phase *before* the event (t+P can contain the
   event's aftermath, e.g. smoke drifting in front of the lamps). The event's own light is whatever
   exceeds the lamps: `out = S_steady + max(0, I_t − lamp_t)`. On ordinary frames, a pixel is an event
   only if it exceeds both the gain model and the phase-matched frame, and a clipped pixel is
   "explained by flicker" if the same lamp phase one cycle earlier clips there too.
   Flow chains out of a flash frame fail the forward-backward check on *appearance* rather than motion;
   where the chained motion is under 1.5 px the alignment is still used. (Before this, the steady
   estimate fell back to `I/G` — the flash divided by a dark-phase gain — leaving amplified-flash patches.)
6. **Do-no-harm guard**: per pixel, the temporal variance of the output over ±6 frames is compared
   with the original's; where processing increased it (ratio > 1.2), the result is blended back toward
   the original (fully at ratio 2). Guarantees the method never *adds* flicker locally — e.g. a steady
   thin object (a glass edge lit by a non-mains source) in front of a 14×-swinging background, which
   every resolution-limited gain estimate tends to give the background's correction.
7. **Exposure guard**: the gain is normalised to its arithmetic mean, so deflickering moves light
   between frames without changing the clip's overall exposure (every working clip lands within
   1.00 ± 0.035). A result more than ~15 % brighter or darker than the original means the flicker
   model failed on that footage, and the original is returned untouched.

**Lessons recorded while building it** (each was a real failure on the shooting-range clip):
- Optical flow on illumination-normalised frames mis-tracks *steady* objects in front of flickering
  backgrounds (normalisation multiplies their contrast on dark phases); flow re-estimated on a
  deflickered pass did not fix the resulting artefact — the do-no-harm guard did.
- "Recover clipped highlights from neighbours" is right for lamps blown out in bright phases and
  catastrophic for a flash (it erased the fireball); clipping must be *explained by flicker* first.
- Every artefact check was done numerically on pixel time-series; downscaled visual previews misled
  more than once.
- A slow-motion clip of falling water (public dataset) came out 55 % brighter: optical flow cannot
  track the drops, so a median pixel had 6 aligned samples for a 10-coefficient fit, and four
  near-Nyquist harmonics 0.01 cycle/frame apart were fitted at once. The fit interpolated the samples,
  the coefficients exploded, and the arithmetic-mean normalisation turned that into a gain of e^−11.
  With the conditioning rule and the support check the same clip goes 67 % → 12 % flicker.

## 4. Evaluation

`bench/` — clips (user footage, the Blind Video Deflickering dataset's slow-motion and time-lapse sets,
and synthetic clips with ground truth from `synth.py`), methods, metrics and renders.

| metric | meaning |
|---|---|
| Flicker % / coverage / peak | residual flicker, FlickerScope at the original's f0 (frozen settings) |
| Warping error ×1000 | mean \|O_t − warp(O_t+1)\| on non-occluded pixels — field standard, independent of our model |
| Structure | SSIM structure term vs original (ghosting, blur, halos) |
| Grain | noise in the strongly flickering zone vs that zone in the original's brightest frames |
| Exposure | mean luminance vs original |
| PSNR vs GT | synthetic clips only |

Baselines: ffmpeg `deflicker`; our earlier per-pixel temporal-median method; All-In-One-Deflicker
(Lei et al., CVPR 2023) with published defaults; and "AIO-improved", AIO's trained neural filter +
local refinement guided by our estimate instead of a per-video atlas. Flickerformer (Qu et al.,
CVPR 2026) can be run with `bench/flickerformer_baseline.py`; its scores are not in these tables yet.

## 5. Results

### FlickerScope vs ground truth (8 synthetic clips, `validate_scope.py`)

Clips: still frames animated with a camera move and a moving occluder, relit by 1–3 simulated sources
(incandescent, magnetic-ballast fluorescent, unfiltered LED driver, LED PWM 100 Hz–2 kHz) on 50/60 Hz
mains, captured at 120/240/480 fps with shutters from 0.25 to 0.98 of the frame time, global or
rolling, with clipping, Poisson-Gaussian noise and 8-bit sRGB quantisation. True flicker 1.2–38.8 %.

| | value |
|---|---|
| flicker frequency correct | 8 / 8 |
| video Flicker %: correlation with truth | **r = 0.988** |
| video Flicker %: mean absolute error | **2.2 percentage points** |
| near-clean clips (true 1.2 %, 3.8 %) | read 1.4 %, 3.7 % |
| per-pixel Percent Flicker map: median correlation | 0.80 |

Known bias: a mild over-read (+0.3 to +7 points), largest with several out-of-phase sources and a
near-full shutter; consistent with estimation noise widening the measured max–min range.

### Shooting-range clip (200 frames, 240 fps capture of 50 Hz lighting, muzzle flash at frame 157)

| method | flicker % (all) | flicker % (calm) | warp err | structure | grain | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original | 29.73 | 29.19 | 58.69 | 1.000 | 0.90 | — |
| ffmpeg `deflicker` | 33.89 | 33.27 | 63.01 | 0.999 | 0.94 | 11 s |
| previous (temporal median) | 8.83 | 8.20 | 18.36 | 0.989 | 0.95 | 45 s |
| AIO (CVPR'23) | 2.28 | 1.59 | 7.53 | 0.754 | 0.75 | ~65 min |
| AIO-improved (our guide) | 2.48 | 1.66 | 8.82 | 0.734 | 0.87 | ~5 min |
| **Flicker Fusion** | **1.98** | **1.24** | **5.21** | **0.986** | **0.68** | ~3 min |

Flicker Fusion has the lowest residual flicker on all frames and on calm segments and the lowest warp
error, while keeping the picture (structure 0.986); AIO rewrites much of it (0.754) and erases the
muzzle flash's fireball. Numbers from `out/range/metrics.json` (17 Oct 2026), every method scored with
the same meter version; they regenerate with `bench/run.py`.
